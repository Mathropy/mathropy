## Problem
Consider a circular necklace with $n$beads. Label the beads with $1,2,\dots, n$, using each number exactly once. For every bead labeled $k$, the sum of the labels on its two neighboring beads must be divisible by $n$.

For example, consider $n=4$. Then $1\to2\to3\to4\to1$ is possible, but $1\to2\to4\to3\to1$, $1\to3\to2\to4\to1$ is not possible.

## Question
- For which values of $n$ does such a configuration exist?
- What are the possible cases?
{: .puzzle-question}

## Hint
<details style="margin-bottom: 1.0rem !important;">
  <summary>Show hint</summary>
  $1\to2\to3\to\cdots\to n\to1$ is one of answers.
</details>

## Answer & Solution
<details>
  <summary>Show solution</summary>
  $1\to2\to3\to\cdots\to n\to1$, $1\to2\to3\to\cdots\to n\to(2n+1)\to(n+1)\to(n+2)\to\cdots\to2n\to1$ are only possible cases. I'll skip the detailed proof for now and add it later.
</details>

> **Mathropy's Note.** The problem looks simple at first, but the classification is surprisingly restrictive.
{: .mathropy-note}
